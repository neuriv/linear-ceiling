Results

The experiments ask different questions. Short replacement measures next-token changes; checkpoint and answer runs follow the model for more tokens; timing runs include the work needed to assemble a reused cache. The measurements below keep those endpoints separate.

1. Replacing cached positions in short excerpts

With Qwen3-1.7B, a fresh receiver cache was computed first. Matching sender key/value states then replaced selected receiver positions; key rotations were adjusted for the receiver positions. Unmatched positions remained freshly computed.

The first eight excerpts used 12 matched positions each. The follow-up selected eight cases with broader matching and replaced 50–95% of the prefix positions.

Total variation measures how much probability mass moved across all next-token choices. These cropped excerpts measure next-token behavior; they do not include end-task scores or reuse timing.

5. Reusing blocks in complete histories

Three full handoff histories were processed with fresh computation or cache reuse for aligned blocks of at least 32 tokens. Missing spans were computed on the cache assembled so far, and timings included matching, key correction, copying, and recomputation of gaps.

HandoffHistory reusedFreshReuseSpeedup

Django 10999 · 6484.1%1.89 s1.01 s1.88×
Django 11066 · 3689.8%2.06 s0.77 s2.68×

Across eight repository, function, or class questions, fresh processing answered 5 correctly and block reuse answered 4. One answer changed from django/django to django; the other seven answer texts matched. Prefix-only reuse preserved every answer text; erasing the history cache scored 0/8, and shuffling reused blocks scored 5/8.

These timings start with tokenized prompts and an already-built sender cache. Building that cache took 5.3–12.0 seconds. Including question processing gave 1.77–2.29× speedups in these runs.
